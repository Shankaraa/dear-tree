# Steward guide (draft)

> **Status: first draft, needs expert review.** This is a plain-language starting point written from general tree-care practice, not from field data. If you have planted and looked after saplings, please correct it, add what is missing for your region, and cite sources. See [issue #17](https://github.com/Shankaraa/dear-tree/issues/17).

A steward is the person on the ground who looks after a Dear Tree for its first 24 months. Most saplings that die, die in the first two years. Your visits are what make the difference.

## Every month

1. **Visit and find the tree.** Check the tag or marker is still there and the tree is where the registry says it is.
2. **Check the water.** Young trees mostly fail from thirst. Is the soil around the base dry a finger-depth down? In dry months, water slowly and deeply rather than a quick splash.
3. **Clear competition.** Pull weeds and grass in a circle around the base so they do not steal water. Keep the trunk itself clear.
4. **Check the mulch.** A layer of leaves or straw around (not touching) the trunk keeps moisture in. Top it up if it has thinned.
5. **Check the guard and stake.** Is the guard intact? Is the stake loose, or rubbing the bark? Fix or replace as needed.
6. **Look at the tree.** Leaves, trunk, new growth, signs of animals or damage.
7. **Take the monthly photo** (see below) and log what you saw.

## The monthly photo

Consistency matters more than beauty, because we compare month to month.

- Stand at the **same spot and angle** every time, ideally with the tag visible.
- Get the **whole tree** in one photo, then one close-up of the leaves and one of the base.
- Shoot in daylight, not against the sun.
- Do not edit or filter.

## Warning signs

Log these and flag them early. Do not wait for next month.

| What you see | What it might mean |
|---|---|
| Leaves wilting or drooping in the morning | Not enough water (or too much) |
| Leaves yellowing across the whole tree | Water stress or poor soil; check drainage |
| Browning leaf edges | Heat or drought stress |
| Chewed leaves or stripped bark | Insects, goats, cattle or rodents; check the guard |
| Trunk leaning or loose in the soil | Stake or roots disturbed; re-firm the soil |
| Bare branches, no new growth in the growing season | Serious stress; the tree may be dying |
| Snapped trunk, or tree gone | Damage or removal; report it |

Local species, soils and seasons differ a lot, so please add what applies where you work.

## When a tree does not make it

Be honest in the log. A dead sapling that is reported can be replaced; one that is hidden cannot. The whole point of Dear Tree is that every named tree is a **real, surviving tree**. A replacement is planted and the 24-month clock starts again for that tree.

## What to log each visit

- Date and your name
- Photos (whole tree, leaves, base)
- Condition: thriving / okay / stressed / dead
- What you did: watered, weeded, mulched, repaired
- Anything unusual
