# Why survival? What the audits say

Dear Tree is built around one claim: **planting a sapling is easy; keeping it alive is the hard part.** This page collects the evidence we have actually read, and says plainly what we do *not* know. Corrections and better sources are welcome ([issue #5](https://github.com/Shankaraa/dear-tree/issues/5)).

## Two audits by India's national auditor (CAG)

Both are about compensatory afforestation plantations funded through CAMPA: government plantations planted to replace forest land cleared for projects. They are not about all tree planting.

### Odisha

*Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (Compliance Audit) for the year ended March 2022*, Chapter IV, "Detailed Compliance Audit on State Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority".

- Auditors jointly inspected **34 plantation sites (3,234 ha)** planted in 2016-22.
- **13 plantations (1,225 ha) had "failed"**, with survival of **7 to 36 per cent**.
- **5 more (733 ha) were "partially successful"**, with survival of **42 to 56 per cent**.
- The state's own rule (Code of Management Plan Procedure, 1990) calls a plantation successful at **60 per cent or more** three years after planting, partially successful at 40-60, and failed below 40.
- One plantation had **7.5 per cent** survival, which auditors attributed to non-maintenance in 2016-17 to 2019-20. The government's reply that elephants damaged it was not accepted by auditors.
- The government accepted the observation (March 2023).
- The report does not say how the remaining 16 sites were classified.

### Uttarakhand

*Performance Audit Report on Functioning of the Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority* (CAG Report No. 5 of 2024), year ended 31 March 2022, para 5.3.

- A third-party report by the Forest Research Institute (March 2021) put the **overall average survival at 33.51 per cent**, against a **mandated 60 to 65 per cent**.
- Auditors' stated reasons in the test-checked divisions: planting among large pine trees, steep and rocky land, and movement of animals and people through the sites. Maintenance funds were collected for 10 years but maintenance was done for only three to five.
- The state's own monitoring wing inspected 395 plantation sites in 2019-22 and reported survival of **1 to 49 per cent in 247 of them**.

## How to read these numbers

- They are **two states, specific years, government compensatory plantations**. Do not generalise to "most trees die" or to all planting.
- Self-reported monitoring by state authorities has reportedly shown higher figures; we have not verified those against a primary source. Different methods give different answers, which is itself a reason for independent, transparent survival reporting.
- The auditors' explanations (maintenance, site choice, grazing) point at **care and place**, not species. That is the premise Dear Tree is built on.

## What we do not know

- There is **no reliable public per-species survival table** for Indian saplings. We will not invent one. Survival in the restoration literature varies mostly with site, seedling size and care.
- We have not verified any national survival figure.
